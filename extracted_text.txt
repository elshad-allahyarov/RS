Abstract. 8
AgI and ZnI2 nanocrystals were key components for AgI-ZnI2-SiO2 hybrid powders 9
(HPs), which could be potentially important for atmospheric artificial precipitation tech- 10
nology. HPs were created by the "Hydrothermal template cocondensation" method 11
(“HTC” method). Mesoporous silica dioxide (MCM48, MCM41, SBA15, SBA16), silver io- 12
dides and zinc iodides were simultaneously grown under specific conditions. Influence of 13
silica dioxide on AgI and ZnI2 nanocrystals characteristics (phase, size and thermal stabil- 14
ity) were studied by various physicochemical analysis methods. In addition to crystal fea- 15
tures, some structural, textural properties AgI-ZnI2-SiO2 hybrid as individual agglomerate 16
and its morphology were determined. It was showed that nanocrystal features were de- 17
pended on synthesis condition. The "chemical pressure" exerted by the pH-former agent 18
had an impact on the initial stages of the process, while the morphology of the silica diox- 19
ide matrix controlled the crystal properties during the post-synthesis phase. The silica di- 20
oxide morphology controlled of crystal properties on the post-synthesis stage. It was es- 21
tablished that thermal stability of AgI and ZnI2 nanocrystals increased due to protective 22
shielding function of that SiO2 matrix. 23

2. Methods or Materials 48
"Hydrothermal template cocondensation" method (“HTC” method) was the basic 49
way for AgI-ZnI2-SiO2 HPs synthesis. The “HTC” method conditions and parameters 50
were established by the authors in previous studies [5-8]. This method allowed to simul- 51
taneously synthesize silicon dioxide, silver iodide (from silver nitrate) and zinc iodide 52
(from zinc nitrate) in the presence of excess potassium iodide. Different types of mesopo- 53
rous silicon dioxide (such as MCM48, MCM41, SBA15 and SBA16) served as a base for the 54
silver iodide and copper iodide crystals. 55
Some substances were used for the synthesis: tetraethoxysilane (TEOS) (98%, Sigma 56
Aldrich), silver nitrate (99%, Sigma Aldrich), zinc nitrate (99%, Sigma Aldrich), potassium 57
iodide (97%, Sigma Aldrich), cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) (98%, Sigma Al- 58
drich), Pluronic P-123, (98%, Sigma-Aldrich), Pluronic F-127, (98%, Sigma-Aldrich),so- 59
dium hydroxide (98%, Sigma Aldrich), Ammonium hydroxide solution (28-30 %, Sigma 60
Aldrich), hydrochloric acid (36%, Sigma Aldrich). 61
AgI-ZnI2-MCM48 HPs synthesis conditions were parameters: hydrothermal aging 62
time was 48 hours, hydrothermal aging temperature was 120 °C, pH of the sol was 10 - 12. 63
Ratio [TEOS] / [CTAB] was 1 : 0.44, ratio [Ag] / [Si] was 0.08. 64
AgI-ZnI2-MCM41 HPs synthesis conditions hybrid were parameters: hydrothermal 65
aging time was 48 hours, hydrothermal aging temperature was 100 °C, pH of the sol was 66
10 - 12. Ratio [TEOS] / [CTAB] was 1 : 0.20, ratio [Ag] / [Si] was 0.08. 67
AgI-ZnI2-SBA15 HPs synthesis conditions were parameters: hydrothermal aging 68
time was 24 hours, hydrothermal aging temperature was 80 °C, pH of the sol was 2-3. 69
Ratio [TEOS] / [Pluronic P-123] was 1 : 0.02, ratio [Ag] / [Si] was 0.08. 70
AgI-ZnI2-SBA16 HPs synthesis conditions were parameters: hydrothermal aging 71
time was 24 hours, hydrothermal aging temperature was 80 °C, pH of the sol was 2-3. 72
Ratio [TEOS] / [Pluronic F-127] was 1 : 0.02, ratio [Ag] / [Si] was 0.08 [5-6]. 73
The ratio [Ag] / [Zn] was 1.00 for new AgI-ZnI2-SiO2 HPs. Then HPs were washed 74
and dried in a drying oven at a temperature of 110 °C. The next step was the annealing 75
stage. This was a post-synthetic treatment to remove the structure-forming agent (CTAB, 76
Pluronic P-123, Pluronic F-127). It took place in a muffle furnace in an air atmosphere at 77
550 °C temperature for 5 hours. 78
The synthesized samples were characterized by various physicochemical methods of 79
analysis. 80
The structure of the metal iodide crystals and powder materials in general was iden- 81
tified by XRD. Diffraction patterns were recorded by an XRD-7000 diffractometer (Shi- 82
madzu, Japan) using CuKα radiation (λav = 1.54 Å). The scan ranges were 1.4 -10.0 and 83
10.0 – 80.0 degrees with a step of 0.010- 0.005 degrees, accumulation time ranged from 1.5 84
to 2.0 s. The diffraction patterns were identified using the JSPDS powder diffraction file. 85
The average crystallite size was calculated by the Selyakov-Scherrer formula: D = K 86
λ/(βcosӨ), where K = 0.89; λ = 1.54056 Å; β - half-width of the reflection (100), rad; Ө - 87
diffraction angle of the reflection, deg. 88
Morphological features of the inorganic hybrid samples were examined using a FEI 89
Quanta FEG650 scanning electron microscope (ThermoFisher Scientific, the Netherlands). 90

The structural and textural properties of HPs were evaluated by low-temperature 91
adsorption of nitrogen with an ASAP 2020 analyzer (Micromeritics, USA) after vacuum 92
degassing of the sample at 350°C for 3 h. The specific surface area of the samples (SBET) 93
and the total pore volume (Vtot) were determined by the BET method, pore size distribu- 94
tion was identified from the desorption isotherms by the BJH method in the pore size 95
range of 1.7 – 300.0 nm. 96
The content of silver iodide and zinc iodide was determined using atomic absorption 97
spectroscopy (Thermo Fisher Scientific iCE 3500, USA) in combination with titration. This 98
approach allowed to clarify the amount of metal iodides in the HPs. Before the measure- 99
ment, silver and zinc were converted from the HPs into a soluble form. The obtained re- 100
sults were recalculated to values reflecting the direct content of metals in the HPs. 101
The thermal behavior of HPs was studied using a TGA/DSC 1 device (METTLER- 102
TOLEDO, Switzerland) in an air atmosphere at a heating rate of 10 °C/min in the temper- 103
ature range of 25–1000 °C. 104
The study of moisture absorption under static conditions was carried out by saturat- 105
ing the powder material with moisture in a desiccator, where the relative humidity of the 106
environment was maintained at about 90%. 107
The HPs' activity was investigated under dynamic conditions. The climatic chamber 108
specially was utilized to create an oversaturated wet area and facilitate its precipitation. 109
This climatic chamber was developed by the Institute of Technical Chemistry of the Ural 110
Branch of Russian Academy of Sciences. The chamber was a hermetically sealed, ther- 111
mally insulated cubic structure with a volume of 8 m³. It was equipped with an ultrasonic 112
humidification system capable of producing 310 cm³/h, a device for spraying HPs, and 113
laser sensors (CDR-10X optical position sensors). The temperature and humidity condi- 114
tions were regulated and maintained by an air conditioning system within the chamber. 115
The experiments were conducted at a temperature of -5 °C. The duration of the destruc- 116
tion process was calculated by subtracting the time when the hybrid was introduced into 117
the oversaturated wet area from the time it became transparent. 118
3. Result and discussion 119
The authors developed some methods for hybrid synthesis and those methods were 120
used for AgI-ZnI2-SiO2 powder creation [5-8]. Those methods allowed to AgI-ZnI2-SiO2 121
formation from different silica dioxide matrix: MCM48, MCM41, SBA15, SBA16. The syn- 122
thesized HPs showed different characteristic features (structural, textural and exploitation 123
properties and thermal stability of nanocrystals) due to different silica dioxide types. 124
It was established that a microagglomerate was structural unit of powders and sam- 125
ples were polydisperse powder. It was shown that the morphology of the HPs’s agglom- 126
erates was determined by the characteristics of the native silica dioxide matrix of the cor- 127
responding family [9, 10]. SEM results showed that AgI-ZnI2-MCM48 HP was worm-like 128
agglomerates, AgI-ZnI2-MCM41 was grape-like agglomerates, AgI-ZnI2-SBA15, AgI- 129
ZnI2-SBA16 were elongated spherical agglomerates (fig. 1). SiO2 type was confirmed by 130
Small-Angle X-ray Scattering. The microagglomerate was a result of stable accumulation 131
of single silica dioxide nanoparticles. It was assumed that electrostatic forces held together 132
silica dioxide nanoparticles. The silver iodide and zinc iodide nanocrystals did not pre- 133
vent self-assembly of powder microagglomerates. 134

thesized hybrids exhibited at least two distinct mechanisms affecting atmospheric mois- 249

served that the condensation activity in relation to atmospheric moisture increased in the 253
sequence MCM41, MCM48, SBA16, and SBA15. This trend was linked to the porosity of 254
the silica matrix, with pore size playing a more significant role than surface area. Conse- 255
quently, the condensation efficiency rose in proportion to the increase in mesoporous sil- 256

rable across all types of AgI-ZnI2-SiO2 HPs. The duration of destruction for AgI-ZnI2- 258
MCM41 was highest, because MCM41 microagglomerates might create stable suspension 259
in atmospheric air. 260
4. Conclusions 261
The hydrothermal template co-condensation method was employed to synthesize 262
hybrid materials composed of AgI and ZnI2 nanocrystals embedded within mesoporous 263
silica dioxide, specifically utilizing MCM48, MCM41, SBA15 and SBA16 as the matrix. The 264
resulting HPs exhibited a well-defined mesoporous structure of silica dioxide, with silver 265
iodide and zinc iodide nanocrystals localized on the silica surface through weak physical 266
interactions. This arrangement was achieved via the synergistic effects of the hydrother- 267
mal process and the templating capabilities of the silica dioxide matrixe, facilitating the 268
controlled deposition and stabilization of the metal halide nanocrystals. 269
It was established that the characterization of the silica dioxide matrix has been 270
shown to significantly influence the morphological attributes of AgI-ZnI2-SiO2 HPs. 271
It was demonstrated that the deposition of silver iodide and zinc iodide nanocrystals 272
onto the surface of silica dioxide matrix leads to a systematic reduction in the specific 273
surface area of the mesoporous SiO2. This reduction was attributable to the filling of the 274
interpore space and pore structures with the deposited nanocrystals, effectively occluding 275
the available surface area. The extent of this reduction in specific surface area varied across 276
different SiO2 matrix as evidenced by the following data: 40 m2/g for MCM48, to 33 m2/g 277
for MCM41, to 27 m2/g for SBA15, to 25 m2/g for SBA16. Furthermore, the analysis re- 278
vealed significant alterations in the pore volume metrics and linear dimensions of the SiO2 279
matrix, irrespective of the matrix type. These findings underscore the profound impact of 280
nanocrystal deposition on the porous structure of silica dioxide, highlighting the need for 281
a comprehensive understanding of the physicochemical processes involved in such trans- 282
formations. The observed changes in pore characteristics suggested that the crystallization 283

process did not only modifying the surface area but also affected the internal architecture 284

It has been shown that the characterization of the structure of mesoporous silicon 286
dioxide did not affect the growth and morphology of silver iodide and zinc iodide nano- 287
crystals. However, the properties of the matrices nd the pH-forming substances, which 288
were used for synthesis, had an indirect effect on crystal formation. 289
It has been found that the morphology and physico-chemical properties of mesopo- 290
rous silicon dioxide play a key role in the formation of the final morphology and sizes of 291
metal halide nanocrystals. 292
The thermal stability of silver iodide and zinc iodide was demonstrated to signifi- 293
cantly enhance due to the protective shielding effect of the silica dioxide matrix. The anal- 294
ysis revealed that after high-temperature exposure, the silver iodide content varied across 295
different mesoporous silica dioxide matrix: specifically, 71 % for MCM48, 68 % for 296
MCM41, 66 % for SBA15, and 64 % for SBA16. This observation underscored the critical 297
role of the SiO2 matrix in preserving the integrity and stability of the iodide-based species 298
under high temperature. 299
The analysis of the AgI-ZnI2-SiO2 operational characteristics revealed differences, 300
which provide a basis for the theoretical classification of novel reagents designed for arti- 301
ficial manipulation of atmospheric moisture. These differences could be potentially uti- 302
lized to optimize their application under diverse atmospheric conditions, including vary- 303
ing cloud water content, altitude, and other relevant meteorological factors. This approach 304
did not only enhance the precision of atmospheric engineering but also broadens the ap- 305
plicability of these reagents in a wide range of environmental contexts. 306
The synthesized reagent demonstrated significant potential to enhance the efficacy of artificial in- 307
terventions in meteorological phenomena. Its implementation is expected to optimize the modula- 308
tion of atmospheric dynamics, thereby improving the controllability of dangerous weather phenom- 309
ena. 310
