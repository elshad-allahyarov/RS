powders

Abstract.   

AgI and ZnI2 nanocrystals were key components for AgI-ZnI2-SiO2 hybrid powders 
(HPs), which could be potentially important for atmospheric artificial precipitation tech-
nology.  HPs  were  created  by  the  "Hydrothermal  template  cocondensation"  method 
(“HTC” method). Mesoporous silica dioxide (MCM48, MCM41, SBA15, SBA16), silver io-
dides and zinc iodides were simultaneously grown under specific conditions. Influence of 
silica dioxide on AgI and ZnI2 nanocrystals characteristics (phase, size and thermal stabil-
ity) were studied by various physicochemical analysis methods. In addition to crystal fea-
tures, some structural, textural properties AgI-ZnI2-SiO2 hybrid as individual agglomerate 
and its morphology were determined. It was showed that nanocrystal features were de-
pended on synthesis condition. The "chemical pressure" exerted by the pH-former agent 
had an impact on the initial stages of the process, while the morphology of the silica diox-
ide matrix controlled the crystal properties during the post-synthesis phase. The silica di-
oxide morphology controlled of crystal properties on the post-synthesis stage. It was es-
tablished that thermal stability of AgI and ZnI2 nanocrystals increased due to protective 
shielding function of that SiO2 matrix.   

Keywords: hybrids; silver iodide; artificial technology; silica dioxide

1. Introduction 

The technology of atmospheric artificial precipitation is strategic sphere to prevent 
hail, drought and heavy rains. Silver iodide is the most well-known substance for “cloud 
seeding”. But it has a number of disadvantages, namely high crystal size, polymorphism 
of crystal lattice, low thermal stability. These parameters are crucial, because silver iodide 
does not contact with ice crystal. Firstly, then silver iodide heated into delivery vehicles, 
it  destroyed  to  metal  silver  and  silver  oxide.  Secondly,  large  native  AgI  crystal  cannot 
interact with icicle. There are common approaches to correct these shortcomings.   

The first approach is to reduce the silver iodide dispersion by changing the parame-
ters of the chemical synthesis of its crystals. The second way is to prevent AgI destruction 
by the addition of iodine donors, such as copper iodide, zinc iodide, magnesium iodide 
or iodate of these metals [1-4]. 

Previously, the authors created a AgI-SiO2 hybrid powder hybrid. The main idea of 
their experimental works was to localize AgI crystals on SiO2 matrix. The authors took 
chemical  reaction  between  silver  nitrate  and  potassium  iodide  as  the  basis  for  the  for-
mation  of  silver  iodide  crystals.  The  synthesis  methods  proposed  by  the  authors  have

2. Methods or Materials 

"Hydrothermal  template  cocondensation"  method  (“HTC”  method)  was  the  basic 
way  for  AgI-ZnI2-SiO2  HPs  synthesis.  The  “HTC”  method  conditions  and  parameters 
were established by the authors in previous studies [5-8]. This method allowed to simul-
taneously  synthesize  silicon  dioxide,  silver  iodide  (from  silver  nitrate)  and  zinc  iodide 
(from zinc nitrate) in the presence of excess potassium iodide. Different types of mesopo-
rous silicon dioxide (such as MCM48, MCM41, SBA15 and SBA16) served as a base for the 
silver iodide and copper iodide crystals. 

Some substances were used for the synthesis: tetraethoxysilane (TEOS) (98%, Sigma 
Aldrich), silver nitrate (99%, Sigma Aldrich), zinc nitrate (99%, Sigma Aldrich), potassium 
iodide (97%, Sigma Aldrich), cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) (98%, Sigma Al-
drich), Pluronic P-123, (98%, Sigma-Aldrich),    Pluronic F-127, (98%, Sigma-Aldrich),so-
dium hydroxide (98%, Sigma Aldrich), Ammonium hydroxide solution (28-30 %, Sigma 
Aldrich), hydrochloric acid (36%, Sigma Aldrich). 

AgI-ZnI2-MCM48  HPs  synthesis  conditions  were  parameters:  hydrothermal  aging 
time was 48 hours, hydrothermal aging temperature was 120 °C, pH of the sol was 10 - 12. 
Ratio [TEOS] / [CTAB] was 1 : 0.44, ratio [Ag] / [Si] was 0.08. 

AgI-ZnI2-MCM41 HPs synthesis conditions hybrid were parameters: hydrothermal 
aging time was 48 hours, hydrothermal aging temperature was 100 °C, pH of the sol was 
10 - 12. Ratio [TEOS] / [CTAB] was 1 : 0.20, ratio [Ag] / [Si] was 0.08. 

AgI-ZnI2-SBA15  HPs  synthesis  conditions  were  parameters:  hydrothermal  aging 
time  was 24 hours, hydrothermal aging temperature  was 80 °C,  pH of the  sol  was 2-3. 
Ratio [TEOS] / [Pluronic P-123] was 1 : 0.02, ratio [Ag] / [Si] was 0.08. 

AgI-ZnI2-SBA16  HPs  synthesis  conditions  were  parameters:  hydrothermal  aging 
time  was 24 hours, hydrothermal aging temperature  was 80 °C,  pH of the  sol  was 2-3. 
Ratio [TEOS] / [Pluronic F-127] was 1 : 0.02, ratio [Ag] / [Si] was 0.08 [5-6]. 

The ratio [Ag] / [Zn] was 1.00 for new  AgI-ZnI2-SiO2 HPs. Then HPs were washed 
and dried in a drying oven at a temperature of 110 °C. The next step was the annealing 
stage.    This was a post-synthetic treatment to remove the structure-forming agent (CTAB, 
Pluronic P-123, Pluronic F-127). It took place in a muffle furnace in an air atmosphere at 
550 °C temperature for 5 hours.   

The synthesized samples were characterized by various physicochemical methods of 

analysis. 

The structure of the metal iodide crystals and powder materials in general was iden-
tified  by  XRD.  Diffraction  patterns  were  recorded  by  an  XRD-7000  diffractometer  (Shi-
madzu, Japan) using CuKα radiation (λav = 1.54 Å). The scan ranges were 1.4  -10.0 and 
10.0 – 80.0 degrees with a step of 0.010- 0.005 degrees, accumulation time ranged from 1.5 
to 2.0 s. The diffraction patterns were identified using the JSPDS powder diffraction file. 
The  average  crystallite  size  was  calculated  by  the  Selyakov-Scherrer  formula:  D  =  K 
λ/(βcosӨ), where K = 0.89; λ = 1.54056 Å; β - half-width of the reflection (100), rad;  Ө - 
diffraction angle of the reflection, deg. 

Morphological features of the inorganic hybrid samples were examined using a FEI 
Quanta FEG650 scanning electron microscope (ThermoFisher Scientific, the Netherlands).

The  structural  and  textural  properties  of  HPs  were  evaluated  by  low-temperature 
adsorption of nitrogen with an ASAP 2020 analyzer (Micromeritics, USA) after vacuum 
degassing of the sample at 350°C for 3 h. The specific surface area of the samples (SBET) 
and the total pore volume (Vtot) were determined by the BET method, pore size distribu-
tion  was  identified  from  the  desorption  isotherms  by  the  BJH  method  in  the  pore  size 
range of 1.7 – 300.0 nm. 

The content of silver iodide and zinc iodide was determined using atomic absorption 
spectroscopy (Thermo Fisher Scientific iCE 3500, USA) in combination with titration. This 
approach allowed to clarify the amount of metal iodides in the HPs. Before the measure-
ment, silver and zinc were converted from the HPs into a soluble form. The obtained re-
sults were recalculated to values reflecting the direct content of metals in the HPs. 

The thermal behavior  of HPs was  studied using a TGA/DSC 1 device  (METTLER-
TOLEDO, Switzerland) in an air atmosphere at a heating rate of 10 °C/min in the temper-
ature range of 25–1000 °C. 

The study of moisture absorption under static conditions was carried out by saturat-
ing the powder material with moisture in a desiccator, where the relative humidity of the 
environment was maintained at about 90%. 

The HPs' activity was investigated under dynamic conditions. The climatic chamber 
specially was utilized to create an oversaturated wet area and facilitate its precipitation. 
This climatic chamber was developed    by the Institute of Technical Chemistry of the Ural 
Branch  of  Russian  Academy  of  Sciences.  The  chamber  was  a  hermetically  sealed,  ther-
mally insulated cubic structure with a volume of 8 m³. It was equipped with an ultrasonic 
humidification system capable  of producing 310 cm³/h, a device  for  spraying HPs, and 
laser sensors (CDR-10X optical position sensors). The temperature and humidity condi-
tions were regulated and maintained by an air conditioning system within the chamber. 
The experiments were conducted at a temperature of -5 °C. The duration of the destruc-
tion process was calculated by subtracting the time when the hybrid was introduced into 
the oversaturated wet area from the time it became transparent. 

3. Result and discussion 

The authors developed some methods for hybrid synthesis and those methods were 
used for  AgI-ZnI2-SiO2 powder  creation [5-8]. Those methods allowed to AgI-ZnI2-SiO2 
formation from different silica dioxide matrix: MCM48, MCM41, SBA15, SBA16. The syn-
thesized HPs showed different characteristic features (structural, textural and exploitation 
properties and thermal stability of nanocrystals) due to different silica dioxide types.   

It was established that a microagglomerate was structural unit of powders and sam-
ples were polydisperse powder. It was shown that the morphology of the HPs’s agglom-
erates was determined by the characteristics of the native silica dioxide matrix of the cor-
responding family [9, 10]. SEM results showed that AgI-ZnI2-MCM48 HP was worm-like 
agglomerates,  AgI-ZnI2-MCM41  was  grape-like  agglomerates,  AgI-ZnI2-SBA15,  AgI- 
ZnI2-SBA16 were elongated spherical agglomerates (fig. 1). SiO2 type was confirmed by 
Small-Angle X-ray Scattering. The microagglomerate was a result of stable accumulation 
of single silica dioxide nanoparticles. It was assumed that electrostatic forces held together 
silica dioxide nanoparticles. The silver iodide and zinc iodide nanocrystals did not pre-
vent self-assembly of powder microagglomerates.

Consequently, potassium iodide indirectly interacted with silver nitrate and zinc nitrate. 
This hypothesis will be further investigated. The formation of copper oxide could provide

The AgI thermal stability was an important feature of AgI-ZnI2-SiO2 reagent. The AgI 
thermal stability was more significant than  ZnI2 thermal stability, because ZnI2 must be 
destroyed under the influence of high temperatures. The thermal destruction of zinc io-
dide helped to prevent the silver iodide sublimation, since iodine donors were formed in 
the  local  area  of  combustion.  The  AgI  thermal  stability  were  studied  by  X-Ray  (fig.  5), 
thermal  gravimetric  analysis  (fig.  6)  and  atomic  absorption  spectroscopy  with  titration 
(table 3). The AgI thermal stability was AgI crystals preservation at the high temperature 
exposure at 1000 °C for 10 minutes in author’s experiments. Of course, the selected mode 
did not fully reflect the combustion temperature of the delivery vehicle. Thus, the authors 
believe that the conditions for studying thermal stability were adequate, and the experi-
mental results were reliable.   

The hybrid samples underwent high-temperature treatment. X-ray analysis of these 
powders revealed that both silver iodide and zinc iodide remained intact in their compo-
sition. Zinc oxide was not detected in all the powders. The presence of silver iodide and 
zinc iodide crystals was consistently observed in all hybrid samples, irrespective of the 
type of silica dioxide matrix (fig. 5). This was confirmed by the characteristic peaks seen 
in the X-ray diffraction patterns.

the dynamics of silver iodide and zinc iodide decomposition under high-temperature con-

SiO2 and ZnI2-SiO2 hybrids, utilizing MCM48, MCM41, SBA15, and SBA16 as silica diox-
ide matrices, were separately exposed to high temperatures. However, these experiments 
did not provide a clear single influence. It was observed and confirmed that an expansion 
of the delayed sublimation zone (Fig. 6) occurred at temperatures ranging from 750 to 900

The analysis of potential operational characteristics (Tables 4) revealed that the syn-
thesized hybrids exhibited at least two distinct mechanisms affecting atmospheric mois-
ture. The first mechanism involved condensation due to the silica dioxide matrix, as evi-
denced by moisture absorption under static conditions. The second mechanism was the

served that the condensation activity in relation to atmospheric moisture increased in the 
sequence MCM41, MCM48, SBA16, and SBA15. This trend was linked to the porosity of 
the silica matrix, with pore size playing a more significant role than surface area. Conse-
quently, the condensation efficiency rose in proportion to the increase in mesoporous sil-

in atmospheric air.     

4. Conclusions   

The  hydrothermal  template  co-condensation  method  was  employed  to  synthesize 
hybrid materials composed of AgI and ZnI2 nanocrystals embedded within mesoporous 
silica dioxide, specifically utilizing MCM48, MCM41, SBA15 and SBA16 as the matrix. The 
resulting HPs exhibited a well-defined mesoporous structure of silica dioxide, with silver 
iodide and zinc iodide nanocrystals localized on the silica surface through weak physical 
interactions. This arrangement was achieved via the synergistic effects of the hydrother-
mal process and the templating capabilities of the silica dioxide matrixe, facilitating the 
controlled deposition and stabilization of the metal halide nanocrystals. 

It  was  established  that  the  characterization  of  the  silica  dioxide  matrix  has  been 

shown to significantly influence the morphological attributes of AgI-ZnI2-SiO2 HPs. 

It was demonstrated that the deposition of silver iodide and zinc iodide nanocrystals 
onto  the  surface  of  silica  dioxide  matrix  leads  to  a  systematic  reduction  in  the  specific 
surface area of the mesoporous SiO2. This reduction was attributable to the filling of the 
interpore space and pore structures with the deposited nanocrystals, effectively occluding 
the available surface area. The extent of this reduction in specific surface area varied across 
different SiO2 matrix as evidenced by the following data: 40 m2/g for MCM48, to 33 m2/g 
for MCM41, to 27 m2/g for SBA15, to 25 m2/g for SBA16.    Furthermore, the analysis re-
vealed significant alterations in the pore volume metrics and linear dimensions of the SiO2 
matrix, irrespective of the matrix type. These findings underscore the profound impact of 
nanocrystal deposition on the porous structure of silica dioxide, highlighting the need for 
a comprehensive understanding of the physicochemical processes involved in such trans-
formations. The observed changes in pore characteristics suggested that the crystallization

ysis revealed that after high-temperature exposure, the silver iodide content varied across 
different  mesoporous  silica  dioxide  matrix:  specifically,  71  %  for  MCM48,  68  %  for 
MCM41, 66 % for SBA15, and 64 % for SBA16. This observation underscored the critical 
role of the SiO2 matrix in preserving the integrity and stability of the iodide-based species 
under high temperature. 

The  analysis  of  the  AgI-ZnI2-SiO2  operational  characteristics  revealed  differences, 
which provide a basis for the theoretical classification of novel reagents designed for arti-
ficial manipulation of atmospheric moisture. These differences could be potentially uti-
lized to optimize their application under diverse atmospheric conditions, including vary-
ing cloud water content, altitude, and other relevant meteorological factors. This approach 
did not only enhance the precision of atmospheric engineering but also broadens the ap-
plicability of these reagents in a wide range of environmental contexts. 

The synthesized reagent demonstrated significant potential to enhance the efficacy of artificial in-

terventions in meteorological phenomena. Its implementation is expected to optimize the modula-

tion of atmospheric dynamics, thereby improving the controllability of dangerous weather phenom-

ena.
